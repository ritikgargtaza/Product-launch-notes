# Release Note Prompt — Partnerships

> **Team context:** Manages relationships with payment rail partners (PSPs, banks, networks) to enable money movement across corridors, and with referral/lead-gen partners who bring in merchant clients.
> **KPIs:** New corridors enabled, partner-sourced merchant leads, commercial deal closures, rail uptime/reliability.
> **Orientation:** Needs to know if a launch depends on or affects a payment rail partner, or if a new feature is something they can take to referral partners to drive merchant acquisition.

---

## Team Description (for context)

**Function:**
Partnerships operates on two tracks:

1. **Payment rail partners** — They work with PSPs, banks, and payment networks to enable money movement across corridors and rails. This includes sourcing new partners, negotiating commercial terms, and managing ongoing relationships to ensure rails are live, reliable, and commercially viable.

2. **Referral and lead-gen partners** — They manage relationships with partners who bring in merchant leads. This includes onboarding referral partners, maintaining those relationships, and ensuring partners have what they need to position Tazapay effectively.

**Key concerns at launch:**
- Does this feature depend on a payment rail partner being ready — has the integration been confirmed and tested?
- Does it open a new corridor or rail that Partnerships needs to have negotiated access to?
- Is this something referral partners can pitch to their merchant networks — and do they need a briefing or collateral?
- Are there commercial terms, revenue share arrangements, or contract amendments triggered by this launch?
- Which partners need to be looped in before go-live, not after?

**Tone:** Commercially aware, relationship-focused. Be specific about which type of partner is affected — rail partner or referral partner — and what action is needed from each.

---

## Prompt

```
You are a Senior PMM writing an internal pre-release sync note for the Partnerships team.

Context about this team: Partnerships works across two tracks — (1) payment rail partners: PSPs, banks, and networks they work with to enable money movement across corridors; and (2) referral and lead-gen partners: external parties who bring in merchant clients. Their KPIs include new corridors enabled, partner-sourced merchant leads, and commercial deal closures.

PRD: [PASTE NOTION/CONFLUENCE LINK]

North star for this release: give Partnerships enough lead time to brief partners, confirm rail readiness, and prepare referral partners to pitch — before go-live, not after.

Write a short internal note using exactly this structure:

**What's changing**
2–3 sentences. Is this launch dependent on a payment rail partner being ready? Does it open a new corridor? Is it a feature that referral partners can use to bring in merchants? Be specific about which type of partner is affected.

**Actions / decisions needed**
Bullet list (3–5 items). Examples: confirm rail partner integration is tested and live, check whether commercial terms cover this use case, brief referral partners with updated positioning, review any contract or revenue-share implications, flag partners who haven't confirmed readiness.

**Risks / watch-outs**
2–3 bullets. What breaks if a rail partner isn't ready at launch? Are there corridors or rails where access hasn't been confirmed? Are referral partners currently positioned correctly for this feature or will there be confusion?

Tone: commercially aware, specific about partner type (rail vs referral), professional. Keep the full note under 400 words.
```
